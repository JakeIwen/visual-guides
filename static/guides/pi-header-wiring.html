Pins 1–20 shown; nothing is plugged into 21–40. Pin 1 sits at the stripe end of the connector, odd pins on one side, even on the other. The BME280 leads take rows 8–10 counted from that end: orange, yellow and green side by side on the even side, red opposite yellow on the odd side. Blue is one row nearer pin 1 than orange.

BME280 in the cab compartment

Purple GY-BME/P280 four-pin breakout, mounted in the compartment above the cab. The Pi reads it by software I²C at 50 kHz, address 0x76, through bme280-mqtt.service.

Blue wire

The single blue jumper sits one row nearer pin 1 than orange, which is pin 14 (ground) on the even side or pin 13 (GPIO27) on the odd side. GPIO27 is an unused input in the Pi's config, so ground is the likely one. Where its far end goes isn't recorded here; it runs down toward the Pi. If it comes loose, trace it before plugging it back in.

Check after replugging

Power the Pi back up, then wait for one reading (the service publishes every 10 s):

ssh vanpi 'timeout 30 mosquitto_sub -h 127.0.0.1 -R -t vanpi/sensors/vanpi_bme280_1/state -C 1'

-R skips the broker's retained last reading, so only a new publication counts. A JSON line with temperature, humidity and pressure means the sensor is wired right. Nothing within 30 s: check the service with ssh vanpi 'journalctl -u bme280-mqtt -n 20'. An I/O error there usually means SDA and SCL are swapped or the sensor lost power.
