Van TV

Use
Play YouTube on the van TV without ads: send a link from the iPhone share sheet, cast from the YouTube app like a smart TV, from Firefox on the Mac, from the vantv CLI, or by pasting a link into the status page. Sound goes HDMI → splitter → optical → Sonos.
Open
Status page vanpi.lan:8795 on the van Wi-Fi, or the Pi's Tailscale address on port 8795 from anywhere on the tailnet (no login; trusted networks only). The Firefox toolbar button opens the sidebar remote.
Kind
A Node service on the Pi (youtube-cast.service) that pairs with YouTube like a TV (DIAL/Lounge) and plays with yt-dlp + mpv; two iPhone Shortcuts; a Firefox extension; a zsh CLI.
Source
~/dev/castit/youtube · JakeIwen/castit (public). Install-specific addresses live in the ignored youtube/local.json.
Status

How it fits

Every sender talks to one receiver on the Pi. The receiver wakes the screen, resolves the stream with yt-dlp, plays it full screen in mpv, and about 1.5 s after each start or resume checks that the Sonos speakers are still grouped for movie sound.

From the phone

iPhone share sheet (recommended)

  1. With Tailscale connected on the phone, open Share in Safari (youtube.com), or any app that shares a YouTube link. No YouTube app needed.
  2. Choose Van TV Queue to append the video to the queue, or Van TV Cast to play it now.
  3. A notification reports Added to the Van TV queue, Casting on Van TV, or the receiver's error text, such as Not a YouTube video link: "…" with a preview of what was shared.

The Shortcuts pass the shared text to the receiver without detecting links themselves. The receiver finds the YouTube link inside it; titles and trailing words are fine. Run either Shortcut directly from the Shortcuts app to use the link on the clipboard.

The Shortcuts call the Pi's Tailscale address, so they work on the van Wi-Fi and away from it, including when the phone is the hotspot. Two things must hold: the Tailscale VPN is on in the phone (its VPN On Demand setting keeps it on), and the Pi has a route to the phone (direct over the van LAN when both are there; the Pi's uplink otherwise, so Starlink or the hotspot must be up). Without Tailscale, use the YouTube app's TV-code route below.

YouTube app

  1. On the van Wi-Fi, open the YouTube app, tap Cast and choose Van TV.
  2. If Van TV isn't listed: iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network → turn on YouTube.
  3. Play anything. The app controls play/pause, seek, volume and its own queue.

When the phone is the hotspot

The phone can't see the Pi on the LAN, so pair once with a TV code. It works over the internet and survives restarts and deploys.

  1. On the Mac, open vanpi.lan:8795 and read Link with TV code (codes refresh every five minutes; Get a fresh code makes a new one).
  2. YouTube app → Settings → Watch on TV → Enter TV code.
  3. Van TV now appears under Cast.

Status page fallback

In Safari: Share → Copy the video's link, open the status page, paste it into YouTube link, then Cast or Add to Queue. Text around the link is accepted here too. Share → Add to Home Screen on that page gives it an icon. Van Wi-Fi, or the Tailscale address.

The app has no speed menu or CC control for Van TV; use the sidebar, status page or vantv for those.

Firefox sidebar

The toolbar button toggles a full-height sidebar. On YouTube, the player's Van TV button casts the current playhead and pauses the page once accepted; Alt+Shift+V does the same for the active tab. Right-click a video link or a watch page for Van TV: Cast Now (Shift: Add to Queue): click to replace playback, Shift-click to play it next, from the start. The same menu offers Save on Van TV, which keeps a copy without interrupting playback. Firefox groups these items into a submenu. The toolbar button is the user-controlled way to close the sidebar; Firefox cannot close it automatically when you switch to a non-YouTube tab.

  9. Played videos (dimmed) sit above the current one; a sliver peeks out so you can scroll up. Click one to replay; × drops it from history. × on Now playing skips to the next queued video and forgets this one (stops if nothing is queued).
  10. Upcoming: click to play now, drag the grip (or Alt+↑/↓) to reorder, ↗ opens YouTube, × removes. ⌘/Ctrl- or middle-click a title opens YouTube in a background tab.
  11. Cast or queue the active tab's video.

Install or update: npm run depf from ~/dev/castit/youtube, click Add in Firefox Developer Edition, reload YouTube tabs.

Saved videos

The receiver keeps saved copies on the Pi's removable exFAT drive at /mnt/EXFAT512/van-tv. The sidebar's Saved section groups them by channel in A–Z order; Play starts from the beginning, Queue adds the video, and Delete asks for an inline confirmation. It shows download progress and says when the drive is unavailable.

WhatBehavior
SaveUse the sidebar button beside CC, Save on Van TV in the Firefox context menu, or vantv download [url|id]. The download runs in the background and fetches only parts not already on disk; repeat it after an error to resume.
Playback sourceA saved copy wins first, then a complete disk cache, then YouTube. Info reports which source is playing; saved copies and complete caches play without network use.
CacheStreaming uses a local cache under van-tv/.cache/. A partly or fully watched video's cache is deleted 10 minutes after the player moves on to something else, unless Download was chosen.
FilesSaved media lives in <Channel>/<Title> [<id>].mp4 or .mkv, with a matching .info.json sidecar.
Drive and spaceIf the drive is absent, playback streams as before and the library is unavailable. Below 2 GiB free, the receiver writes nothing and refuses downloads; existing saved or complete cached videos still play.

Deleting a saved video removes its media and sidecar without interrupting an already open local playback. The cache is removed after a successful save; a download that is still using the original stream waits until that playback closes.

Queue and history

WhatBehavior
Add to QueueInserts next, from the start (link timestamps ignored). Starts at once if the player is idle or stopped. Up to 50.
OrderThe cast queue plays before the phone app's own queue, at the end of each video or on Next.
Played historyThe last 20 videos that started, any source, oldest first. Replaying one starts it from zero.
Remove and clear× removes any row: played, now playing (skips to the next queued video, or stops) or upcoming. Clear empties history and the upcoming queue; the current video keeps playing.
Queue/history persistenceQueue and history are saved on the Pi and survive Stop, phone disconnects and restarts. Restoring them never starts playback.
TitlesFrom YouTube's oEmbed lookup; mpv's title once a video loads.
PrefetchThe next queued video is resolved in the background, so it starts faster than a first cast.

Sound and picture

SettingRangeNotes
Volume0–100mpv's own volume, shared with the phone's volume buttons. Changing it unmutes. Trims YouTube's louder mastering without touching the Sonos level.
Speed0.25–3×Pitch kept. Needs proportionally more bandwidth and decoding.
A/V offset−1…+1 sPositive plays sound later. Use minus when voices come after lips move.
QualityAuto, 1080/720/480/360Explicit choices cap height at ≤30 fps. Auto keeps the Pi-tuned H.264 selection.
Subtitleson/off + languageUploaded captions preferred, then automatic ones. The phone's CC button can't control these.

Video is drawn with OpenGL (--vo=gpu, like VLC's GLES output). The older xv output let frames back up on the 180°-rotated screen: audio ran ahead and Pause left the picture moving for a second.

Sonos grouping

Sonos undoes the movie grouping by itself when optical audio starts again after a pause, leaving sound on vonMid only. So instead of setting it up before playback, everything checks it after:

  • About 1.5 s after each start and resume, Van TV runs sns.sh ensure_rear_movie. The video library (VLC, including the BTT play/pause button) does the same.
  • Healthy (vonMid on TV input, playing, muted; rear pair grouped to it and unmuted): no changes.
  • Broken: it repairs the grouping and keeps the current rear volume.
  • The manual BTT rear_movie button still works and sets volume 47.

The Sonos scripts live in ~/dev/scripts (shared/python/sonos_tasks.py, pi/sns.sh); Van TV only names the command in local.json's roomCommand.

vantv CLI

vantv [youtube-url]                      # cast; default: front Chrome/Safari tab, then clipboard
vantv pause|resume|stop|status
vantv queue [youtube-url|list|clear]
vantv download [youtube-url|id]              # keep video; default: currently loaded video
vantv saved                                  # list saved videos by channel
vantv vol [+n|-n|n|mute|unmute]
vantv speed [+x|-x|x]                    # e.g. vantv speed 1.5 · vantv speed +0.25
vantv delay [+ms|-ms|ms]                 # A/V offset
vantv quality [auto|1080|720|480|360]
vantv subs [on|off|<lang>]

Alias it to ~/dev/castit/youtube/vantv.zsh. It reads local.json beside it, or a base URL in VAN_TV.

HTTP API

JSON POSTs need Content-Type: application/json and no foreign Origin; web pages can't call it.

EndpointBody / result
GET /api/statusPlayer state, video, position, settings, queue, played, canPlayNext, diagnostics, source, current-video download, library.available, pairing code; while a video loads, loading: {"phase": "resolve"|"start"|"buffer", "elapsed": s, "expected": s|null}.
POST /api/cast{"videoId": …} or {"url": "https://youtu.be/…?t=90"}, optional position and title. 202 while loading.
POST /api/controlaction: pause, resume, toggle, stop, next, previous, seek, audio_delay, speed, volume, mute, quality, subtitles, download (optional id), delete_saved (id). Download returns when accepted; poll status/library for completion.
POST /api/queueaction: add (videoId or url, optional title), play (key), remove (key, or current: the playing video's id), move (key, index), clear (upcoming and history).
GET /api/library{available, freeBytes, channels, downloads}; channels are case-insensitively sorted and videos are newest saved first. With no drive, freeBytes is null and channels is empty.
GET /healthSupervisor health, no pairing data.

Deploy

cd ~/dev/castit/youtube
npm test                                  # whole castit suite
npm run depr                              # receiver → Pi (restarts it; ends a playing cast)
npm run depf                              # Firefox extension → install prompt
bash receiver/deploy.sh status            # health, unit, PID
bash receiver/deploy.sh update-ytdlp      # when YouTube breaks extraction
  • youtube/local.json (ignored) holds the Pi's SSH target, LAN and optional Tailscale addresses (tailscaleAddress; the status port binds it, retrying until Tailscale is up), audio device, roomCommand and the sender URLs, Tailscale first so the Shortcuts use it. The receiver deploy copies it to the Pi.
  • To enable saved videos, set receiver.videoStore to /mnt/EXFAT512/van-tv in that private config and install ffmpeg on the Pi with sudo apt-get install ffmpeg. The receiver still starts and streams when the drive is absent; below 2 GiB free it writes nothing and refuses downloads.
  • The daily exFAT snapshot excludes van-tv/; Borg already skips the removable drive, so saved videos are not in those backups.
  • Releases live in /home/pi/youtube-cast/releases/; the deploy prints the retained previous release for rollback.
  • Pairing identity, settings, queue and history live in ~/.local/state/youtube-cast/ on the Pi. Never delete it to update.
  • Optional YouTube watch history: zsh firefox/push-youtube-cookies.zsh (--remove turns it off). Uses account cookies; your call.

~15 s before a cast startsyt-dlp stream lookup (~10–14 s) on a busy PiQueued videos are prefetched. The sidebar shows the stage; the Video load timing journal line shows each phase.
Loads take 30 s or more; journal says path=hookThe stream lookup failed, so mpv looks the video up again itselfUsually a one-off (a new YouTube player script). Every load on path=hook means yt-dlp's output changed: bash receiver/deploy.sh update-ytdlp, then check the resolver.
Nothing plays after about a minute; journal shows resolve=60000ms and outcome=failedThe stream lookup hit its 60 s limit (slow uplink or a busy Pi). The receiver gives up rather than start the same lookup again in mpvCast again; the retry is usually much faster. Check the Pi's load (ssh vanpi uptime) and the uplink.
